> 6. How to query physical NIC link speed, duplex mode, and transceiver optical diagnostics via Linux `ethtool`

## Real-Life Analogy

Think of Layer 1 (Physical Layer) as **the physical road and track infrastructure of a global transit network**:

- **Layer 1 (The Paved Asphalt & Steel Rails):**
  - Layer 1 does not understand street names, vehicle registration numbers, or who is driving. It only provides the physical medium (**copper wires or glass fiber strands**) capable of conducting electrical pulses or laser light beams (**0s and 1s**).
- **Copper Twisted Pair (Standard Asphalt Road):**
  - Inexpensive and easy to install for short suburban trips (<100 meters), but speed and distance are limited by friction and electrical resistance.
- **Single-Mode Optical Fiber (High-Speed Maglev Vacuum Tube):**
  - An ultra-pure, ultra-narrow laser vacuum tunnel capable of propelling light pulses across hundreds of kilometers at near the speed of light with virtually zero signal loss.
- **Transceiver (The Engine Coupling Adapter):**
  - The mechanical converter that translates digital computer instructions into focused laser wavelengths (e.g., 850nm or 1310nm light pulses) plugged directly into the switch chassis.

### 2. Single-Mode Fiber (SMF) vs Multi-Mode Fiber (MMF)

```text
   Multi-Mode Fiber (OM4 - 50µm Core): Multiple Light Modes (Modal Dispersion)
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ ╲   ╱ ╲   ╱ ╲   ╱ ╲   ╱ ╲   ╱ ╲   ╱ ╲   ╱ ╲   ╱ ╲   ╱  │ (Light rays bounce at
   │───╳─────╳─────╳─────╳─────╳─────╳─────╳─────╳─────╳────│  different angles;
   │ ╱   ╲ ╱   ╲ ╱   ╲ ╱   ╲ ╱   ╲ ╱   ╲ ╱   ╲ ╱   ╲ ╱   ╲  │  limits max distance)
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

   Single-Mode Fiber (OS2 - 9µm Core): Single Direct Light Mode (Zero Modal Dispersion)
   ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │════════════════════════════════════════════════════════│ (Straight laser beam;
   │                                                        │  travels tens of km)
   └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Multi-Mode Fiber (MMF):** Wide core allows multiple light paths (modes). Cheaper LED/VCSEL light sources, but suffers from **modal dispersion** (pulses spread out over time), capping distance at ~100–400m.
- **Single-Mode Fiber (SMF):** Tiny 9-micron core allows only a single focused laser mode to propagate. Eliminates modal dispersion, allowing data rates of **400G/800G over 40+ kilometers**.

### 3. Signal Modulation: NRZ vs PAM4

As datacenter speeds increased from 10G to 400G and 800G, hardware reached the physical clock limit of traditional **NRZ (Non-Return-to-Zero)** modulation:

- **NRZ Modulation (2 Levels):** 1 bit per symbol (0 or 1). At 25 Gbaud, throughput is 25 Gbps.
- **PAM4 (Pulse Amplitude Modulation 4-Level):** 4 distinct voltage/light levels (00, 01, 10, 11). Transmits **2 bits per symbol**, doubling throughput to 50 Gbps at the exact same baud rate.

## Hands-On Lab: Querying Physical Interfaces & Optical Diagnostics

```bash
# Step 1: Query physical network interface capabilities, speed, and duplex status
sudo ethtool eth0 2>/dev/null || ip link show

# Step 2: Inspect interface driver, firmware version, and bus location
sudo ethtool -i eth0 2>/dev/null || echo "Driver info not available"

# Step 3: Read optical transceiver Digital Diagnostic Monitoring (DDM / DOM)
# (Displays laser transmit/receive optical power in dBm and temperature)
sudo ethtool -m eth0 2>/dev/null || echo "Transceiver optical diagnostics (SFP/QSFP)"

# Step 4: Check physical Layer 1 link carrier status
cat /sys/class/net/eth0/carrier 2>/dev/null || echo "1 (Link UP)"

# Step 5: Check physical error counters (CRC errors, frame alignment errors)
ip -s link show dev eth0
```

## Common Interview Questions

**Q1: What is the fundamental difference between Single-Mode Fiber (SMF) and Multi-Mode Fiber (MMF)?**

**Single-Mode Fiber (SMF)** has a tiny core diameter (approximately 9 microns) that allows only a single ray (mode) of infrared laser light (typically 1310nm or 1550nm) to propagate. Because there is only one path, SMF suffers zero modal dispersion, enabling transmission distances up to 40–80 km at extreme speeds (400G/800G). **Multi-Mode Fiber (MMF)** has a much wider core (50 or 62.5 microns) that allows multiple light rays to bounce through the core simultaneously using inexpensive 850nm VCSEL lasers. However, modal dispersion causes signal degradation over distance, restricting MMF to short datacenter runs under 400 meters.

**Q2: What is the purpose of SFP+, QSFP28, and OSFP optical transceivers?**

Optical transceivers are hot-pluggable hardware modules that interface a switch or server PHY chip with optical fiber or copper cables. **SFP+ (Small Form-factor Pluggable)** supports single-lane 10 Gbps speeds. **QSFP28 (Quad SFP28)** combines 4 parallel 25 Gbps lanes to deliver 100 Gbps aggregate throughput. **OSFP (Octal Small Form-factor Pluggable)** combines 8 parallel 50G or 100G PAM4 lanes to deliver **400 Gbps and 800 Gbps** throughput for AI/ML GPU clusters and next-generation spine-leaf cloud fabrics.

**Q3: What causes CRC (Cyclic Redundancy Check) errors on a physical network interface?**

CRC errors occur when the 32-bit Frame Check Sequence (FCS) calculated by the receiving NIC does not match the checksum sent in the Ethernet frame trailer, indicating that bits were corrupted in transit across the physical Layer 1 medium. Root causes include: damaged or bent optical fiber patch cables, dirty optical fiber end-faces, out-of-spec copper cable length (>100m for Cat6a), electromagnetic interference (EMI) from nearby electrical motors/power lines, or failing optical transceiver laser diodes.

**Q4: How does PAM4 modulation double network throughput compared to traditional NRZ?**

Traditional **NRZ (Non-Return-to-Zero)** modulation uses two distinct voltage or optical intensity levels (High and Low) to represent binary `1` and `0`, encoding exactly 1 bit of information per clock cycle (symbol). **PAM4 (Pulse Amplitude Modulation 4-Level)** uses four distinct signal levels (e.g., 0V, 1/3V, 2/3V, 1V) corresponding to binary states `00`, `01`, `10`, and `11`. Because each symbol carries **2 bits instead of 1**, PAM4 delivers double the bit rate at the identical baud (clock) frequency.

**Q5: What is Auto-Negotiation and what happens during a Duplex Mismatch?**

Auto-Negotiation (IEEE 802.3u) is a Layer 1 protocol where two connected Ethernet interfaces exchange Fast Link Pulses (FLPs) to advertise and agree upon the highest mutually supported speed (e.g., 1000 Mbps) and duplex mode (Full vs Half). If one side is manually hardcoded to Full-Duplex and the other side is set to Auto-Negotiate, auto-negotiation fails, and the auto side defaults to Half-Duplex (**Duplex Mismatch**). The Full-Duplex side transmits freely, while the Half-Duplex side detects collisions, aborts transmission, and sends jam signals, causing massive packet drops, late collisions, and severe throughput collapse.
