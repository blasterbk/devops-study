# Why Should You Learn Compaction?

One of the most common Kubernetes interview questions is:

> **Why do Kubernetes watches sometimes fail with "410 Gone"?**

Or,

> **Why can't etcd keep every revision forever?**

Or,

> **What happens to old MVCC revisions?**

The answer is:

```text
Compaction
```

Compaction is one of the most important maintenance mechanisms inside etcd.

Without compaction:

- Database size grows forever.
- Memory usage increases.
- Disk usage continuously grows.
- Watch recovery becomes slower.
- Storage performance degrades.

Compaction keeps etcd healthy.

---

> **Important:** Compaction is **irreversible**. Once a revision has been compacted, the historical state of all keys at that revision is permanently deleted from etcd. Any Watch client that tries to resume from a compacted revision will receive HTTP 410 Gone. Design Watch retry logic (Informer pattern) to handle this gracefully.

# What is 410 Gone?

HTTP:

```text
410 Gone
```

means:

> **The requested historical resource is no longer available.**

In Kubernetes,

this usually means:

```text
Requested Watch Revision

Has Been Compacted
```

It is **not** an indication that the object itself has been deleted.

---

# Why Not Return Empty Data?

Suppose:

Watch requested:

```text
Revision 100
```

```text
1000
```

History removed.

Returning:

```text

```

would be dangerous.

The client would incorrectly believe:

```text
Nothing Happened
```

Instead,

the server forces recovery.

# Production Compaction Strategy

Production clusters typically:

- Run automatic compaction.
- Retain enough history for normal watch recovery.
- Monitor watch failures.
- Ensure Informers correctly handle relisting.
- Balance storage usage against historical retention.

Choosing an extremely short retention period may increase watch restarts.

Choosing an extremely long retention period increases storage requirements.

# Common Misconceptions

### ❌ Misconception 1

Compaction deletes Kubernetes objects.

Wrong.

It removes historical revisions, not current objects.

---

### ❌ Misconception 2

Compaction is the same as a snapshot.

Wrong.

Snapshots create backups.

Compaction removes old MVCC history.

---

### ❌ Misconception 3

410 Gone means the object was deleted.

Wrong.

It usually means the requested historical revision has been compacted.

---

### ❌ Misconception 4

Compaction immediately frees disk space.

Wrong.

Compaction removes obsolete MVCC history logically.

Physical disk reclamation requires defragmentation.

---

### ❌ Misconception 5

Watches permanently fail after compaction.

Wrong.

Clients perform a fresh LIST and establish a new WATCH.

---

### ❌ Misconception 6

Historical reads always work.

Wrong.

Only revisions newer than the compaction point remain available.

# Common Interview Questions

### Q1. Why does etcd need compaction?

Because MVCC continuously creates new revisions, and without compaction the database history would grow indefinitely.

---

### Q2. What does compaction remove?

Historical MVCC revisions that are older than the chosen compaction revision.

---

### Q3. Does compaction delete current Kubernetes objects?

No.

It removes only obsolete historical versions.

---

### Q4. What is automatic compaction?

A background process where etcd periodically removes old revisions according to its retention policy.

---

### Q5. What is manual compaction?

A maintenance operation initiated by an administrator to compact history up to a specified revision.

---

### Q6. Why do watches sometimes fail after compaction?

Because the client requested a revision that no longer exists in the retained MVCC history.

---

### Q7. What causes HTTP 410 Gone?

The requested watch revision has already been compacted and cannot be replayed.

---

### Q8. How do Kubernetes Informers recover from 410 Gone?

They perform a fresh LIST to rebuild their cache and then establish a new WATCH from the latest state.

---

### Q9. Is compaction enough to reduce disk usage?

No.

Compaction removes obsolete history logically, while defragmentation is required to reclaim physical storage space.

---

# Troubleshooting Guide

| Problem | Likely Cause |
|----------|--------------|
| Frequent `410 Gone` responses | Watch clients are reconnecting after revisions have already been compacted |
| Historical reads fail | Requested revision is older than the compaction point |
| Database continues growing after compaction | Physical storage has not yet been defragmented |
| Informers repeatedly relist | Long disconnections, slow processing, or aggressive compaction settings |
| High storage consumption | Compaction retention too long or defragmentation not performed |
