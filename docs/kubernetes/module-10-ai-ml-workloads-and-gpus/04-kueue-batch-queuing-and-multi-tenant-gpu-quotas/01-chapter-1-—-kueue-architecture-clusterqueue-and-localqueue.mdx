# Chapter 1 — Kueue Architecture: ClusterQueue & LocalQueue

> **Goal:** Master Kubernetes-native batch scheduling, job queuing, and multi-tenant quota management for AI/ML workloads using **CNCF Kueue** — understanding why vanilla Kubernetes schedulers suffer from catastrophic gang-scheduling deadlocks, dissecting the structural CRD hierarchy (**`ResourceFlavor`**, **`ClusterQueue`**, **`LocalQueue`**, and **`Workload`**), and orchestrating the automated **Job Suspension $\to$ Admission** lifecycle for PyTorch, Ray, and batch Jobs.
>
> By the end of this chapter, you will understand:
> 1. Why vanilla Kubernetes batch scheduling causes GPU starvation and job deadlocks
> 2. The structural relationship between ClusterQueue (Global quota) and LocalQueue (Namespace queue)
> 3. How `ResourceFlavor` models hardware tiers (`on-demand-a100`, `spot-h100`) via node labels
> 4. How Kueue uses `spec.suspend: true` to hold jobs in queue until 100% of required GPUs are free
> 5. How the `Workload` Custom Resource tracks scheduling priority and admission gates
> 6. How to author production `ClusterQueue`, `LocalQueue`, and Kueue-managed batch Jobs

## Real-Life Analogy

Think of CNCF Kueue as **the automated maître d' and reservation queue manager at an ultra-exclusive Michelin 3-star restaurant with only 16 dining seats**:

- **The Vanilla Kubernetes Chaos (The Uncontrolled Stampede):** 50 large families (**50 Distributed PyTorch Training Jobs requiring 8 GPUs each**) arrive at the restaurant simultaneously.
  - In vanilla Kubernetes, there is no queue manager. Everyone rushes through the door at once.
  - Family A grabs 4 chairs. Family B grabs 4 chairs. Family C grabs 4 chairs. Family D grabs 4 chairs (**All 16 GPU seats are occupied**).
  - But every family requires **8 chairs together** to eat (**Distributed Gang-Scheduling requirement**).
  - Result: All 4 families sit at half-empty tables staring at each other for hours. Nobody can eat, no meals are served, and all 50 families deadlock (**100% GPU Starvation & Zero Throughput**).
- **The Kueue Solution (The Professional Maître d'):**
  - Kueue stands at the entrance and holds everyone in the lobby (**`spec.suspend: true`**).
  - The maître d' checks the master table availability (**`ClusterQueue`**).
  - Family A is called: *"Table for 8 is ready (**Job Admitted - `suspend: false`**)."*
  - Family A eats their meal, finishes training in 30 minutes, and leaves.
  - The maître d' immediately seats Family B.
  - Result: 100% table utilization, zero deadlocks, and maximum throughput!

## 1. Defining Hardware Tiers (`ResourceFlavor`)

```yaml
apiVersion: kueue.x-k8s.io/v1beta1
kind: ResourceFlavor
metadata:
  name: nvidia-h100-flavor
spec:
  nodeLabels:
    nvidia.com/gpu.product: "NVIDIA-H100-SXM5-80GB"
  nodeTaints:
    - key: "nvidia.com/gpu"
      value: "present"
      effect: "NoSchedule"
```

---

## 2. Defining Global Quota Pools (`ClusterQueue`)

```yaml
apiVersion: kueue.x-k8s.io/v1beta1
kind: ResourceFlavor
apiVersion: kueue.x-k8s.io/v1beta1
kind: ClusterQueue
metadata:
  name: cluster-gpu-queue
spec:
  namespaceSelector: {}                # Accessible by all authorized namespaces
  queueingStrategy: BestEffortFIFO     # Admits smaller jobs if head-of-line job cannot fit!
  resourceGroups:
    - coveredResources: ["nvidia.com/gpu", "cpu", "memory"]
      flavors:
        - name: nvidia-h100-flavor
          resources:
            - name: "nvidia.com/gpu"
              nominalQuota: 16         # Total 16 GPUs in this physical cluster pool!
            - name: "cpu"
              nominalQuota: "128"
            - name: "memory"
              nominalQuota: "512Gi"
```

## 3. Creating Tenant Entry Point (`LocalQueue`)

Deploy this into the developer's team namespace:

```yaml
apiVersion: kueue.x-k8s.io/v1beta1
kind: LocalQueue
metadata:
  name: nlp-team-queue
  namespace: team-nlp
spec:
  clusterQueue: cluster-gpu-queue      # Binds to global ClusterQueue!
```

## 4. Submitting a Queued PyTorch Job to Kueue

Attach the **`kueue.x-k8s.io/queue-name`** label to any standard batch `Job`, `RayJob`, or `PyTorchJob`:

```yaml
apiVersion: batch/v1
kind: Job
metadata:
  name: bert-large-pretraining
  namespace: team-nlp
  labels:
    # Binds the Job to the local team queue!
    kueue.x-k8s.io/queue-name: nlp-team-queue
spec:
  suspend: true                        # Kueue keeps job suspended until quota is free!
  parallelism: 2
  completions: 2
  template:
    spec:
      restartPolicy: OnFailure
      containers:
      - name: trainer
        image: nvcr.io/nvidia/pytorch:24.04-py3
        command: ["python", "train.py"]
        resources:
          limits:
            nvidia.com/gpu: 4          # 2 pods x 4 GPUs = 8 GPUs total
            memory: "64Gi"
```

## Common Interview Questions

**Q1: Why does vanilla Kubernetes batch scheduling fail for multi-node distributed AI training jobs?**

Vanilla Kubernetes lacks **Gang-Scheduling (All-or-Nothing Scheduling) and Job Queuing**. When multiple multi-node GPU training jobs are submitted concurrently, the `kube-scheduler` schedules individual pods incrementally. If Job A and Job B both require 8 GPUs on a cluster with only 8 GPUs total, the scheduler may assign 4 GPUs to Job A and 4 GPUs to Job B. Because distributed PyTorch/NCCL training requires **all worker pods to be online simultaneously to initialize collective communication**, both jobs hang indefinitely, consuming 100% of cluster GPUs without performing any computation.

**Q2: How does Kueue use the `spec.suspend` field to prevent GPU deadlocks?**

When a batch Job or PyTorchJob is submitted with a `kueue.x-k8s.io/queue-name` label, Kueue's mutating webhook automatically sets **`spec.suspend: true`**. The Kubernetes `kube-scheduler` ignores suspended jobs completely, preventing partial pod scheduling. Kueue's central admission controller monitors the `ClusterQueue`. Only when **100% of the requested GPUs, CPUs, and memory are simultaneously available** does Kueue admit the job and set `spec.suspend: false`, allowing all worker pods to be scheduled as a single unified gang.

**Q3: What is the difference between `queueingStrategy: StrictFIFO` and `BestEffortFIFO` in Kueue?**

- **`StrictFIFO`**: Jobs are evaluated in strict priority/timestamp order. If the job at the head of the queue cannot fit (e.g. requires 16 GPUs when only 8 are free), the queue **blocks completely**, preventing smaller downstream jobs from running until the head job is admitted.
- **`BestEffortFIFO`**: If the head-of-line job cannot fit, Kueue scans downstream and admits smaller jobs that *can* fit within remaining idle quota, maximizing GPU hardware utilization without starving large jobs indefinitely.

**Q4: What is a `ResourceFlavor` in Kueue, and how does it abstract heterogeneous compute?**

A `ResourceFlavor` represents a distinct tier of physical hardware (e.g. `on-demand-h100`, `spot-a100`, `standard-cpu`). It associates the flavor with Kubernetes node labels and tolerations. Inside a `ClusterQueue`, administrators can define quota across multiple flavors in priority order, allowing Kueue to attempt scheduling a training job on cheap Spot GPUs first and falling back to On-Demand GPUs if Spot capacity is unavailable.

**Q5: Which AI/ML job frameworks are natively supported by CNCF Kueue?**

Kueue natively integrates with the entire cloud-native AI ecosystem via dedicated integration webhooks, including: (1) Standard `batch/v1` Jobs; (2) **Kubeflow Training Operator** (`PyTorchJob`, `TFJob`, `MPIJob`, `XGBoostJob`); (3) **KubeRay** (`RayJob`, `RayCluster`); (4) **JobSet** (multi-template batch jobs); and (5) **AppWrapper** (IBM AI workloads).
